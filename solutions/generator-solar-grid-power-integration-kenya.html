Solar + Battery + Grid + Generator Power Integration in Kenya

Stop treating solar, batteries, the grid and the generator as separate systems. Smartech designs the electrical and control layer that coordinates available sources around the site's critical loads, operating priorities and backup requirements.

The customer problem

Many facilities have a generator but still experience avoidable downtime because the generator, ATS, solar inverter and batteries were installed as separate projects. Common complaints include “the generator does not start when the battery is low,” “solar goes off when the grid fails,” “the generator starts even when the battery has power,” and “we cannot see what is powering the building.”

The solution is an engineered source-control strategy with clear priorities, protection, metering and monitoring.

Typical source-priority logic

Solar

Use available PV generation for active loads and, where the system is designed for it, charge the battery.

Battery

Supply selected loads during outages or scheduled periods according to state-of-charge and operating limits.

Grid

Supply normal demand when available and within the site's configured operating strategy.

Generator

Start when grid conditions and battery state-of-charge or load requirements justify generator operation.

ATS / controls

Coordinate source transfer, interlocking, start/stop signals, stabilisation delays and safe return-to-mains sequences.

Monitoring

Show source status, load, battery SOC, energy flows, alarms and runtime so operators can see what is happening.

Battery monitoring and automation

Battery monitoring should go beyond displaying a battery percentage. Depending on the battery and inverter architecture, useful data may include state-of-charge, charge/discharge power, voltage, current, temperature, alarms, cycle information and available capacity.

Automation can use these values to create rules such as: start the generator if the grid is unavailable and battery SOC reaches a configured threshold; stop the generator after the battery recovers or grid power returns; keep critical loads powered while non-critical loads are shed. Exact thresholds and control logic must be commissioned for the installed equipment.

What the monitoring dashboard should answer

  • Which source is powering the load right now?
  • How much solar is being generated?
  • How much power is the building using?
  • What is the battery state-of-charge?
  • Is the battery charging or discharging?
  • Why did the generator start?
  • How long has the generator been running?
  • Are there active inverter, generator, ATS or battery alarms?
  • How much energy came from solar, grid, battery and generator?

Where this solution is valuable

Factories, offices, hospitals, schools, hotels, warehouses, farms, telecom sites, security installations and remote facilities can benefit when power continuity, fuel reduction or remote visibility matters.

For grid-connected solar systems, interconnection and net-metering requirements must be checked against the current Kenyan regulatory framework and the specific utility connection.

Questions customers ask before calling

Can solar and a generator run together?

Some hybrid systems can coordinate solar and generator operation, but compatibility depends on inverter capability, generator controls, protection, operating mode and the required sequence. The design must prevent unsafe source interaction.

What happens when the battery gets low?

The control system can be configured to issue an alarm, transfer selected loads, start a generator or change operating priority, depending on the equipment and the site's agreed strategy.

Can I see the generator and battery from my phone?

A monitoring system can provide remote visibility where the generator controller, inverter/BMS, meters and communications network expose the required data. The dashboard should be designed around useful alarms and decisions, not just raw numbers.
