What customers usually search for when a generator fails

People rarely begin with a technical specification. They search the symptom they can see: “generator is not starting,” “generator starts then stops,” “generator is overheating,” “generator is not producing power,” “ATS is not changing over,” “generator battery keeps going flat,” or “generator is using too much fuel.”

These are high-intent service searches. The website should answer the symptom first, explain likely causes, then give the customer a clear next step: inspection, diagnostic visit, maintenance contract or repair.

  • Generator will not start / hard starting
  • Generator starts but shuts down
  • Generator runs but does not take load
  • Low or unstable generator voltage
  • Generator overheating
  • Generator battery not charging
  • ATS fails to transfer during an outage
  • Generator starts unexpectedly
  • Generator consumes excessive fuel
  • Generator needs regular servicing or a maintenance contract

Generator + grid + solar + battery: one power strategy

A generator should not be treated as an isolated machine when a site already has solar, batteries and grid power. The better question is: which source should power which load, under which condition, and who or what controls the change?

A properly engineered hybrid arrangement can use solar during available production, battery storage for selected loads and ride-through, grid power when available, and the generator as a controlled backup. Automatic transfer and control logic can use source availability, load demand and battery state-of-charge to decide when the generator should start or stop. The exact sequence must be designed and commissioned for the equipment installed.

Monitoring

Source status, load in kW/% , voltage, current, frequency, battery state-of-charge, solar generation, generator runtime, alarms and energy consumption can be brought into a dashboard where the hardware supports it.

Automation

Typical functions include mains-failure start, transfer after generator stabilisation, battery low-state generator start, generator cool-down, load prioritisation and alarm notifications.

Maintenance contracts that prevent emergency call-outs

For factories, offices, hospitals, schools, hotels, warehouses and other critical facilities, generator maintenance should be planned around operating hours and the consequence of failure—not just a calendar reminder.

  • Routine visual and mechanical inspection
  • Engine oil, filters and coolant checks according to the manufacturer's schedule
  • Battery, charger and starting-system checks
  • Alternator and AVR/control checks
  • ATS functional testing
  • Load testing where appropriate
  • Alarm and protection verification
  • Service records and recommendations for corrective work

Questions customers ask before calling

Why does my generator start and then stop?

Common causes include low oil/coolant protection, fuel delivery problems, battery or starter issues, overload, overheating, controller alarms or a sensor fault. A diagnostic check should identify the shutdown cause before parts are replaced.

Why is the generator running but not powering the building?

The fault may be at the generator output, breaker/protection, ATS, changeover controls, cabling or load side. The transfer sequence and source voltages should be tested as a system.

Can a generator work with solar and batteries?

Yes, but the integration depends on the inverter, generator controller, battery system, protection and operating mode. The system needs a deliberate control sequence rather than simply connecting sources together.

Can you monitor a generator remotely?

Where the generator controller, meters and communications equipment support it, monitoring can report operating status, electrical measurements, alarms, runtime and other available data to a local or remote dashboard.
