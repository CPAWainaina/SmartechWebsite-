Electrical and Automation Integration for Kenyan Factories

Factories increasingly need electrical systems and automation to be designed as one operating environment. Power quality, control, communications and maintenance are closely connected.

Why integrate electrical and automation engineering?

Control equipment depends on stable power, appropriate protection and reliable communications. Separating these disciplines too early can produce difficult troubleshooting and inconsistent documentation.

Integrated scope

  • LV distribution and protection
  • Motor control and starters
  • VFDs and soft starters
  • PLC and remote I/O
  • HMI and SCADA
  • Industrial Ethernet and fibre
  • UPS and backup power
  • Electrical safety testing

What should be documented?

Keep panel drawings, cable schedules, network diagrams, I/O lists, PLC/HMI backups, equipment registers and commissioning records aligned. This becomes critical when the original installer is no longer available.

Questions & Answers

Frequently asked questions

Can one contractor handle power and automation?

Yes, an integrated engineering contractor can coordinate the interfaces, while specialist approvals and regulated work remain subject to the relevant licences and competence.

Why use fibre in industrial networks?

Fibre can provide long-distance connectivity and electrical isolation advantages in appropriate environments.

Does automation improve energy efficiency?

It can, when control strategies reduce unnecessary runtime, improve process control and enable measurement.

What is the first step?

A site survey and review of the existing electrical, control and network architecture.

Need help with your site or equipment?

Share the equipment type, location, photos and last inspection or maintenance date. Smartech can advise on the appropriate technical scope.
