Variable Frequency Drives for Pumps in Kenya: Benefits, Risks and Design Considerations

Variable frequency drives, also called VFDs or variable speed drives, control motor speed by changing the frequency and voltage supplied to the motor. They are widely used for pumps and fans where process demand varies.

Why use a VFD on a pump?

A pump does not always need to operate at full speed. A correctly selected drive can match motor speed to process demand, improve pressure control and reduce mechanical stress associated with repeated direct-on-line starting.

Where VFDs add value

  • Variable water demand
  • Booster systems
  • Irrigation
  • Process pumping
  • HVAC pumps and fans
  • Remote pumping systems with monitoring

What can go wrong?

Wrong sizing, poor ventilation, harmonics, motor incompatibility, incorrect acceleration settings, cable issues and inadequate protection can cause nuisance trips or premature failure. Drive design should therefore consider the motor, load, environment, control method and protection.

VFD and automation integration

A VFD can be integrated with PLC, HMI or SCADA so operators can monitor speed, current, faults and process variables and change setpoints within the approved control strategy.

Questions & Answers

Frequently asked questions

Does a VFD always save energy?

No. Savings depend on the load profile, pump/system design and operating strategy. It is particularly useful where speed can be reduced to match variable demand.

Can a VFD control a pump automatically?

Yes, with suitable sensors and control logic it can maintain pressure, level or flow targets.

What size VFD do I need?

Selection depends on motor rating, current, duty, overload requirements, supply and application. Motor nameplate data should be reviewed.

Can you integrate VFDs with PLCs?

Yes, depending on the drive and communication protocol, VFDs can be integrated with PLC/HMI/SCADA systems.
