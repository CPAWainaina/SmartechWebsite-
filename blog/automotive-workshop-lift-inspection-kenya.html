Automotive Workshop Lift Inspection in Kenya: Garage and Fleet Safety Guide

Automotive workshops rely on two-post lifts, four-post lifts, hydraulic systems, compressors, electrical tools and other machinery. The safety of the workshop depends on the condition of each asset and on disciplined inspection and maintenance.

Why vehicle lift inspection matters

A vehicle lift carries people and high-value vehicles above ground. Wear, poor maintenance, unsafe modifications or damaged safety devices can create unacceptable risk. Workshop owners should combine manufacturer maintenance requirements with the applicable Kenyan statutory requirements.

Pre-inspection checks

  • Rated capacity and identification plate
  • Columns, arms, locks and visible structural condition
  • Hydraulic hoses, cylinders and connections
  • Safety locks and mechanical restraint systems
  • Controls and emergency stop functions
  • Electrical isolation and wiring condition
  • Level and floor/foundation condition
  • Previous inspection and maintenance records

Inspection readiness for fleet operators

For organisations with multiple workshops, standardise equipment registers, inspection dates, defect categories and corrective action. This creates a central view of overdue equipment and recurring failures.

Questions & Answers

Frequently asked questions

Do vehicle lifts need inspection in Kenya?

Where the equipment falls under plant requiring statutory examination, the owner should arrange the applicable examination and retain the required documentation.

What should a garage check before inspection?

Check identification, rated capacity, structural condition, locks, hydraulics, controls, electrical isolation, housekeeping and maintenance records.

Can you inspect a multi-branch fleet workshop?

Yes, we can plan standardised site assessments and reporting across branches subject to scope and access.

Can you fix electrical defects?
